# My bot's knowledge file
Notes to myself: all notes come from the Girl Eat World guide "Complete Japan Itinerary and Travel Guide for First Timers: 10 Days in Japan" (last updated September 19, 2026). Prices are in yen and may change.

## Do I need a visa to enter Japan, and what is Visit Japan Web?
Japan lets visitors from more than 70 countries enter without a visa, so the first step is checking whether your passport is on that list. If it isn't, you apply before you travel, usually in person at a Japanese embassy, though some countries can use an online e-visa. The source also recommends completing immigration and customs steps online before arrival through Visit Japan Web, a free service that makes arrival faster. It can also provide a QR code for tax-free shopping. Always confirm current entry rules on official government sites before booking.

## How many days should I spend in Japan?
The source recommends at least 10 days to get the essential Japan experience, which fits Tokyo, Kyoto, Nara, and Osaka. If time is short, five days can still work for a first visit: stay in Tokyo and add a short trip to Hakone or Mount Fuji. The author did exactly that on her first trip, spending five days in Tokyo plus a day and a half in Hakone, and it was enough to make her want to return many times. The right length depends on what you want to do.

## What is the 10-day Golden Route for Japan?
The Golden Route is the source's suggested first-timer itinerary. Spend days 1 to 3 in Tokyo, days 4 to 5 in either Hakone or Fuji Five Lakes (pick only one), days 6 to 7 in Kyoto, day 8 in Nara, and days 9 to 10 in Osaka. The author suggests using Osaka as a base for Kyoto and Nara, since hotels there tend to be cheaper and trains run often. She also suggests booking an open-jaw flight, such as arriving in Tokyo and leaving from Osaka, to avoid backtracking.

## How much cash should I bring to Japan, and where can I get yen?
Japan is still largely cash-driven, especially at small restaurants and outside big cities. The source suggests about 5,000 yen per person per day for daily needs, and more if your hotel isn't prepaid or you plan to shop. Convenience store ATMs, especially 7-Eleven, work with foreign cards and charge about 110 to 220 yen per withdrawal. When the machine offers a choice, pick the yen amount so your own bank does the currency conversion, since ATM exchange rates are poor.

## Do you tip in Japan?
No. Tipping is not practiced or expected in Japan, and the source says it can confuse locals or even come across as rude or condescending. This applies at restaurants, hotels, and taxis, even though the service is excellent. If a traveler asks whether to leave a tip to thank someone, the answer is to skip it and express thanks verbally instead.

## What are the main etiquette rules in Japan?
Japanese culture puts a lot of weight on not bothering other people. The source advises keeping quiet on trains, putting your phone on silent, and not taking calls on public transport. Take your shoes off when entering homes and some restaurants. Public trash cans are rare, so carry your trash back to your hotel. Eating while walking is considered impolite, though festival food stalls are an exception. In an onsen (hot spring), you must be completely naked, since swimsuits and underwear are seen as unhygienic.

## How do I handle my luggage when moving between cities in Japan?
The source lists several options. Takuhaibin is a luggage delivery service that sends bags to your next hotel or the airport, so you can travel hands-free. Coin lockers at major train stations cost roughly 300 to 700 yen depending on size, but they fill up fast and are usually for one day only. Tourist information centers and hotels will often store bags too. Pack and drop your luggage off early in the day, because lockers can sell out at busy stations like Kyoto.

## How do I get around Japan?
Between cities, the Shinkansen (bullet train) is the most popular choice, and domestic flights can be cheaper for long distances. Within cities, local trains work best, and you can pay by tapping an IC card such as Suica, Pasmo, or ICOCA. These cards work across cities, can pay for items at convenience stores and vending machines, and can also be set up on an iPhone. Taxis are expensive, but useful late at night or with children. To drive in Japan, you must have an international driver's license.

## How does tax-free shopping work in Japan?
Tourists can shop tax-free at participating stores for purchases over about 5,000 yen, saving the 10% consumption tax on the spot. You need to bring your passport, and look for the tax-free symbol, since rules vary by store and even by branch. Consumables like food and cosmetics are sealed in a bag you can't open until you leave Japan. The source notes that Japan plans to switch to a tax refund system starting November 1, 2026, where tourists get their money back when exiting the country. Check current rules before shopping.

## When is the best time to visit Japan?
The most popular times are spring cherry blossom season (mid-March to early April) and autumn leaf season (mid to late November). Winter is cold but sunny, and it offers the best chance of seeing Mount Fuji. Summer is hot and humid, and August is the hottest month, which the source suggests avoiding. Typhoon season runs from July to October. Also watch for busy holidays: Golden Week (the first week of May) and Obon (August 13 to 16) bring heavy domestic travel and higher crowds.

## How much does a trip to Japan cost?
The source gives rough estimates rather than a full budget. Convenience store meals run about 300 to 1,000 yen, regular restaurant dishes about 1,500 to 2,500 yen, and high-end restaurants about 8,000 to 10,000 yen per person. Coffee is around 600 to 800 yen. For lodging, the author budgets about 20,000 yen a night for a decent hotel for two people. Train travel within a city costs about 800 to 1,000 yen per day. The total depends on cities, group size, and travel style.

## How do I get from the airport into Tokyo?
The source recommends flying into Haneda if possible, since it is closer to the city center. From Haneda, you can take a local train to the station nearest your hotel. From Narita, take the Narita Express or the Keisei Skyliner, whichever is closer to your hotel. A private car transfer is a good option for groups or travelers with lots of luggage, but it costs more for solo travelers. If you book one, make sure the car has room for all your bags.

## What are the best tips for first-time visitors to Japan?
The source's top tips: get a data eSIM so you have internet without swapping SIM cards, and book popular attractions like Shibuya Sky and teamLab about a month ahead since tickets can sell out. Wear comfortable walking shoes, because you'll walk a lot. Download offline Google Maps for each city and the Japanese Google Translate pack, which can translate photos of menus and signs. Complete Visit Japan Web before you arrive to speed up immigration.
